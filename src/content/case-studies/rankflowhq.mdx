## Summary

SEO teams juggle chat prompts, keyword tools, optimisers and docs, and still struggle to publish consistently good pages at volume. I built **RankFlowHQ** to run the whole program in one guided pipeline: keyword → clustering → research → outline → **cited** draft → SEO packaging. I owned every part: the problem, positioning, scope, AI design, UX and the build. It's live at [app.rankflowhq.com](https://app.rankflowhq.com/), with an [Education Hub](https://education.rankflowhq.com/) that runs the same engine on education news.

## Context

I spent 5 years building SEO and content platforms at Collegedunia and KollegeApply, including scaling a site to 300K+ indexed pages. I saw the same pattern everywhere: AI made *writing* cheap, but **publishing good pages consistently** was still slow, because research, structure, accuracy and on-page standards lived in people's heads and in scattered tools.

## The problem

- **Fragmented workflow:** keyword research in one tool, briefs in docs, drafts in a chat window, optimisation in a plugin. Every handoff costs time.
- **Inconsistent quality at volume:** each writer and each prompt produces a different structure, so rankings suffer as output scales.
- **Trust:** generic AI writing confidently invents statistics and facts. An SEO page with a fabricated number is a liability, not an asset.

<Callout type="insight">
Users didn't need another way to *generate text*. They needed a **repeatable process** that produces pages they can trust and publish. The product is the pipeline and its standards; the model is one component.
</Callout>

## Users

- **Bloggers and niche site owners:** publish quality pages faster without juggling tools.
- **SaaS content teams:** turn product expertise into SEO pages on a steady cadence.
- **Agencies:** standardise production across clients to improve turnaround and margins.

## Options considered

<Options>
  <Option title="A better prompt library on top of ChatGPT">
    Fast to build, but it doesn't fix the workflow: no research step, no structure standards, no packaging, and no protection against invented facts. Rejected: it competes with a free tool on that tool's own ground.
  </Option>
  <Option title="An optimisation score for existing pages (like Surfer)">
    Useful, but it only tunes pages that already exist. Users still have to research and write. Rejected as the core, though a visibility grader became a supporting tool.
  </Option>
  <Option title="An end-to-end, grounded production pipeline" chosen>
    Harder to build, but it owns the whole job from keyword to publish-ready page, and grounding gives a clear quality edge over generic AI writing. Chosen.
  </Option>
</Options>

## What I built

<Figure src="/products/rankflowhq.webp" alt="RankFlowHQ homepage: type a keyword, get a researched, SEO-ready article" caption="The live homepage: one keyword box, one button, a full article in about 3 minutes" />

**1. A staged pipeline instead of a chat box.** Every article goes through the same stages: keyword and intent-aligned supporting terms → clustering → SERP research → outline → draft with citations → SEO packaging (title, meta description, FAQs, internal links). Fixed stages make quality repeatable and debuggable: when an output is weak, I can see *which stage* failed.

<Callout type="decision">
**Ground the draft in retrieved research, with citations.** The model writes from sources the pipeline fetched, not from its memory. This is the main defence against invented facts, and citations let users check claims instead of trusting them.
</Callout>

**2. One job on the homepage.** The first version led with a platform pitch and a no-signup teaser demo. The current homepage does one thing: type a keyword, press "Write my article", and get a long-form draft with sources, an SEO title and a meta description in about 3 minutes. A full sample run (keywords → outline → article) sits below it so people see the output before they start.

<Callout type="decision">
**From a teaser demo to a real result behind a free account.** The teaser showed a fragment, which proved the idea but not the value. Now a free account (email only, no card) unlocks 5 full articles that are saved to a dashboard. That's enough to judge the real output, and it gives me an account to measure activation and convert to Pro at $49/month.
</Callout>

**3. Tools around the core job.** A **bulk article agent** runs the pipeline on many topics; **repurpose from URL** rewrites an existing page into a new article; and a free **AI SEO toolkit** (keyword clustering, visibility grader, off-page and outreach helpers) brings in search traffic that leads to the writer.

**4. Dogfooding in a vertical I know.** The [Education Hub](https://education.rankflowhq.com/) applies the engine to education news: RSS and sitemap aggregation, a Google Trends explorer, AI repurposing and indexable news pages. Education is the domain I've worked in for 5 years, so I can judge output quality like an expert.

## Architecture: the AI pipeline

<PipelineDiagram />

Each article runs through eight steps. Every step catches its own errors and falls back to a simpler result, so one failed call degrades the article instead of killing it.

| # | Step | What happens | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keywords | Two Google searches plus a Gemini call turn the topic into primary and supporting keywords | Simple keyword set from the topic |
| 2 | Research | Two Tavily searches (the topic, and "statistics" for it): up to 16 sources, deduplicated | Article proceeds with less evidence |
| 3 | SERP | Top 8 Google results, "People also ask", related searches, featured snippet | Outline relies on research only |
| 4 | Queries | Gemini clusters the questions people search | Uses related searches + PAA directly |
| 5 | Outline | Gemini writes a fixed structure: one H1, 5–6 H2s with 2–3 H3s each, 5 FAQs, conclusion | A stub outline |
| 6 | Draft | Gemini writes the article, streamed live, citing statistics inline and ending with a Sources section | Error shown inline |
| 7 | SEO package | Title, meta description and schema from the finished draft | Article saved without the package |
| 8 | Enrich (optional) | Comparison tables, images and charts for up to 10 sections | Skipped |

## Model choices

<Callout type="decision">
**Gemini first, with an automatic fallback chain.** The draft prompt can carry up to 14,000 characters of research plus a 12,000-character outline, so a long-context, low-cost model fits best. When a model is rate-limited (HTTP 429), the code waits under a second and tries the next Gemini model, so a busy model doesn't fail the user's run.
</Callout>

- **Why not one big premium model?** Cost per article matters on a free plan with 5 articles. A cheaper long-context model with good research inputs beats an expensive model writing from memory.
- **Why keep Groq and OpenRouter at all?** A "multi" mode runs them side by side with Gemini on the same inputs. It's my comparison bench for choosing models, not the default path.
- **Prompt budget is explicit.** Research, outline and search-query blocks are each capped and marked as truncated, so long inputs never silently push out the instructions.

## Guardrails: grounding, quality and cost

**Grounding.** The model gets the research block and must cite statistics inline as *[Source: domain]* and end with a Sources section of at least four authoritative links.

**Quality gate.** Every draft can be scored by a rule-based checker before publishing:

| Check | Rule |
|---|---|
| Length | Error below 900 words |
| Keyword stuffing | Error above 3.2% primary-keyword density |
| Structure | Warning below 3 H2 sections; warning if any section is under 120 words |
| Usefulness | Warnings for fewer than 2 tables or a missing FAQ schema |
| Linking | Flags under 2 external or under 3 internal links |
| AI tone | Flags common AI-cliché phrases |
| Duplicates | Warns when a draft's heading skeleton matches an earlier draft |

**Cost and abuse controls.** Monthly runs per plan (guest 2, free 5, Pro 300), hourly limits per user, sign-in required for paid routes, and a stricter hourly cap on the article step itself. Users who bring their own Gemini key skip the monthly quota, which keeps heavy users from costing me money.

## Human in the loop

The AI writes a strong first draft; a person decides what gets published. The product is designed around that split:

1. **Draft:** the pipeline streams the article live, so the user sees it being written instead of waiting on a blank screen.
2. **Edit:** the draft opens in a rich-text editor. The homepage and FAQ tell users to treat it as a first draft and add their own experience, examples and opinions, because that's what makes content rank and convert.
3. **Check:** a rule-based quality gate scores the draft for length, keyword stuffing, thin sections, missing tables or FAQ schema, weak linking, AI-cliché phrasing and duplicate outlines. The server can block a save when the draft fails.
4. **Publish:** the user saves to their dashboard, exports Markdown or DOCX, or copies it out.

<Callout type="decision">
**Why keep a human in the loop at all?** The model can't know the user's real experience, and search engines reward first-hand detail. Positioning the output as a first draft sets the right expectation and puts the final judgement with the person accountable for the page.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tradeoff">
**The gap I found:** the quality gate exists on the server, but the editor doesn't run it when a user saves. Today the "check" step depends on the user's own judgement. Wiring the gate into the editor, showing the issues inline, and blocking publish only on errors (not warnings) is the first fix on the roadmap.
</Callout>

## Evaluation: what exists, and what I found missing

When I audited my own pipeline for this case study, I found gaps that matter more than any new feature:

- **Citations are requested, not verified.** Nothing checks that the cited sources were actually in the research, or that the claims match them.
- **No offline test set.** Quality is judged by the rule-based gate and by reading drafts, not against a fixed set of keywords.
- **No model logging.** The app doesn't record which model produced which article, so a quality change after a fallback can't be traced.
- **No timeouts on external calls.** A slow research or model call can hang a step.

<Callout type="insight">
The quality gate checks the *shape* of an article. It can't tell whether the facts are right. For an AI writing tool, that's the gap that decides whether users trust it.
</Callout>

**The evaluation plan, before the next feature:**

| Metric | How it's measured | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Citation validity | Share of inline citations whose domain appears in the research sources | ≥ 95% |
| Unsupported claims | Statistics in the draft with no matching research snippet (sampled, human-checked) | ≤ 1 per article |
| Gate pass rate | Drafts with no quality-gate errors on a fixed set of 30 keywords (typical, hard, adversarial) | ≥ 90% |
| Fallback rate | Runs where Gemini fell back to a second model, from new per-run logs | Tracked weekly |
| Cost per good article | Model + research cost divided by drafts that pass the gate | Tracked, then reduced |
| Time to draft | Keyword to finished draft | ~3 minutes, p90 under 5 |

## Trade-offs

<Callout type="tradeoff">
**Grounding vs. speed.** Live web research adds time and cost to every article. I accepted that for the full pipeline, because one invented statistic can sink a page.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tradeoff">
**Breadth vs. depth as a solo builder.** I chose a few connected tools (pipeline, bulk agent, clustering, grader) over many shallow features, and used AI-assisted coding to build faster than one person normally could.
</Callout>

## Mistakes and what I learned

- **My first demo showed a fragment, not the value.** The no-signup teaser proved the idea but not the output, so visitors left without seeing a real article. I replaced it with 5 full free articles behind an email-only account. **Lesson:** a demo has to show the actual result people will pay for.
- **I changed what my main domain means.** rankflowhq.com now hosts my local-SEO agency, and the AI writer moved to app.rankflowhq.com. Every old product URL redirects, but links and rankings built on the old pages take time to carry over. **Lesson:** decide early what a domain is for; moving products later costs search equity.
- **I don't yet publish usage numbers.** I set up tracking late, so this case study has no activation or conversion data. **Lesson:** instrument the funnel before launch, not after.
- **Distribution is part of the product.** Building it was the easier half; getting the right users to try it is the harder one. That's why the homepage was cut down to a single action, and why the free tools and the Education Hub exist.
- **The pipeline is the moat, not the model.** Models improve for everyone. Research quality, structure standards and grounding are what make the output consistently publishable.
- **Being my own first user is an advantage.** Years of running SEO platforms meant I knew which outputs would actually rank and which would only look good.

## What's next

- Close the gaps from the audit: run the quality gate inside the editor, add an automatic citation check against the research sources, log which model wrote each article, and add timeouts on every external call.
- Build the 30-keyword test set and publish the first evaluation report (gate pass rate, citation validity, unsupported claims, cost per good article).
- Track activation and conversion: the % of new accounts that finish a first article, and how many of those upgrade to Pro after the 5 free ones.
- Learn from user edits: feed corrected drafts back as examples to improve future output.
