## Summary

**Kundli Jyoti** is a Vedic astrology app live at [kundlijyoti.com](https://kundlijyoti.com/). It computes kundli, kundli matching and dasha timelines from real planetary positions, then explains them in plain language. An assistant called **Ask Jyoti** answers follow-up questions. It installs as a PWA, supports family profiles and shares to WhatsApp.

## The problem

Astrology is a huge category in India, and most apps follow the same playbook: show a frightening dosha, then sell a ₹5,000 gemstone or a paid call. Users can't tell what was calculated and what was invented. Generic AI chatbots make it worse, because they will happily make up a planetary placement.

<Callout type="insight">
The opportunity wasn't a better horoscope. It was a reading people could trust, from an app that wasn't trying to scare them into paying.
</Callout>

## Who it's for

Indian users who want a kundli, a marriage match or a daily rashifal without a sales pitch: young adults checking compatibility, families keeping profiles for parents and children, and regular rashifal readers.

## Architecture

1. **Input:** birth date, time and place stay in the browser.
2. **Compute:** the app calculates planetary positions, houses, nakshatra and dasha on the device.
3. **Interpret:** a rule-based engine turns positions into readings; an AI model can rewrite them in friendlier language but can't change the facts.
4. **Share:** results become a WhatsApp card and a shareable image, and family profiles are saved on the device.

## Key product decisions

<Callout type="decision">
**Computed, not guessed.** Every reading starts from calculated planetary positions. The language model only explains the result, so it can't invent a placement. Ask Jyoti runs on a rule-based astrologer, and the LLM layer is added on top only when a provider is configured.
</Callout>

<Callout type="decision">
**Privacy as the pitch.** The kundli is computed on the device and birth details stay there. In a category known for upselling, trust is the main point of difference.
</Callout>

## Mistakes and what I learned

- **I only set up Google.** Bing Webmaster Tools shows zero impressions for Kundli Jyoti, while my anime site gets real visitors from Bing. Bing matters in India, so this was free traffic I left on the table. **Lesson:** submit every site to Google *and* Bing on launch day.
- **I published the festival page too late.** The Bhai Dooj page went up close to the festival and still became the top page. Google needs a few weeks to rank a page, so an earlier page would have caught far more searches. **Lesson:** publish festival pages 3–4 weeks ahead, using a festival calendar.
- **Most pages sit on page 2.** The average position is 15.5 and the CTR is 0.6%. People rarely click past page 1. **Lesson:** improve fewer pages, especially the ones already close to page 1, before adding more.
- **Festival traffic is a spike, not a base.** Visits went from 42 to 8 the day after Bhai Dooj. **Lesson:** pair each festival page with pages people need every day (panchang, choghadiya, rashifal), so the site keeps a floor between festivals.
- **People search in Hindi as well as English.** Several top queries are in Hindi or Hinglish ("kab hai", "कैसा रहेगा"). **Lesson:** the full app needs Hindi, not just the navigation.

## What's next

- Measure the pilot: weekly users, return rate and reports requested.
- Track whether the Choghadiya pages lift the daily baseline between festivals.
- Turn on the ₹199 report once retention justifies it.
- Add Hindi-first readings and more regional languages.
