## Summary

**PayHours Calc** is a set of fast, private calculators for US hourly workers and small-business payroll, live at [payhourscalc.com](https://payhourscalc.com/). It covers time cards, hours, overtime, hourly-to-salary, pay raises, military time and 2026 military pay.

<ProductDeck slug="payhourscalc" />

## The problem

People searching for a time card or overtime calculator land on slow, ad-stuffed pages. Many get the edge cases wrong: overnight shifts that cross midnight, unpaid breaks, or state overtime rules that differ from the federal rule.

## Choosing the niche

<Callout type="decision">
**Keyword data before code.** I picked the niche from Google Keyword Planner data: about 900K US searches a month across the cluster, low ad competition, and weak existing results for pages like the pay raise calculator.
</Callout>

<Options>
  <Option title="One big all-in-one calculator">
    Simpler to build, but it would rank for almost none of the specific searches people type.
  </Option>
  <Option title="Focused calculators plus programmatic pages" chosen>
    Ten calculators, each matched to a search intent, plus 200+ generated pages such as "$25 an hour is how much a year" that answer the exact question.
  </Option>
</Options>

## Getting the details right

- Overtime rules for all 51 jurisdictions (50 states and DC), not just the federal rule.
- Pay tables for 25 military grades.
- Overnight shifts and breaks handled in the time card calculator.

## Built for speed

<Callout type="decision">
**Static and fast.** Every page is pre-rendered and served from Cloudflare's edge. The calculators load instantly, run in the browser with no account or upload, and hosting costs almost nothing.
</Callout>

<Callout type="tradeoff">
Static pages mean no saved history or accounts. For a quick calculation, speed and privacy matter more than saving.
</Callout>

In its first week, PayHours went from nothing to about **1,300 impressions a day** and **5,990 in total**, with 320 pages indexed. The programmatic pages did their job: "$150 an hour is how much a year" and "$55k a year is how much an hour" ranked around **position 8–10**, on page one.

Then on **30 October impressions fell to almost zero**, and nothing had been deployed since 28 October. My other new site, AniLumi, dropped within a day of it. My best explanation is that Google gives brand-new sites a short burst of visibility to test them, then pulls back until they earn trust. The 4 clicks from 5,990 impressions (0.1% CTR) show the test didn't go well: pages were shown, but rarely chosen.

These numbers are tiny, and I'm showing them because they're honest. Two weeks after launch, Bing had shown PayHours 51 times and sent no clicks yet. The signal is in *what* people searched: almost all of it was **military minutes conversion charts**: tables that turn minutes into hundredths of an hour for payroll, at an average position around 6. That's a narrower, more specific need than the broad "hours calculator" searches I built for.

**Next step:** a dedicated, printable military-minutes conversion chart page, since that's the search showing up first, and Google Search Console tracking for the same pages to see whether the pattern holds there.

## Mistakes and what I learned

- **I treated the first week as a trend.** A new site's early impressions are Google testing it. Real performance shows up over months, so I now judge launches on 60+ days of data.
- **Ranking isn't the same as being chosen.** Pages reached page one but earned a 0.1% click-through rate. Titles and descriptions need to promise the exact answer, like "$150/hour = $312,000/year", not a generic calculator.
- **I almost shipped placeholder tax numbers.** Three states (Arizona, Maine, South Carolina) first used a temporary standard-deduction value. I caught it by checking every figure against the official state sources, then added the citation to each state page. **Lesson:** for money calculators, every number needs a source on the page.
- **A CDN setting changed who could crawl the site.** Cloudflare's managed robots.txt blocks AI crawlers by default. I only noticed after launch. **Lesson:** check robots.txt on the live site, not just in the code.
- **I can't show real traffic yet.** PayHours lives in a separate Cloudflare account, and I hadn't switched on its visitor analytics, so I only have Search Console data. **Lesson:** turn on analytics before the first visitor, in the same place as your other sites.
- **I picked the wrong Cloudflare project type at first.** The first deploy failed because the setup tried to run a server for a fully static site. **Lesson:** match the hosting model to the build output before deploying.
