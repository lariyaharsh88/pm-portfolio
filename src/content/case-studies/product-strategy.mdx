## Summary

I had evenings, weekends and no ad budget. So instead of betting everything on one idea, I ran my independent products like a portfolio: score each opportunity on the same few questions, launch the cheapest ones first to get a signal quickly, write down what I will *not* build, and move effort to whatever the data says is working.

This case study is about the decisions, not the code.

## The constraint

- **Time:** one person, around a full-time PM job.
- **Money:** no paid acquisition. Every product had to find users through search or my own content.
- **Risk:** most ideas fail. The goal was to find out *which* ones quickly and cheaply, not to polish one product for months.

<Callout type="insight">
When you can't afford to be wrong slowly, the best strategy is to be wrong quickly and cheaply, and to know in advance what result would make you stop.
</Callout>

## How I scored opportunities

Every idea answered the same five questions before I wrote any code:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Is there proven search demand?** | No ads means search is the channel; no demand means no users |
| **Is the competition weak?** | Thin, slow or spammy results are a gap a small product can win |
| **Do I have an edge?** | Domain knowledge, my own content, or data others don't use |
| **How fast can I get a signal?** | A static site can launch in days; a marketplace can't |
| **Is there a path to revenue?** | Ads, affiliates or a paid tier, even if switched on later |

## The bets

| Product | Demand signal | Edge | Time to signal | Revenue path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PayHours Calc** | High: ~900K US searches a month across the cluster, low ad competition | Getting state rules and edge cases right | Days (static site) | Ads, payroll affiliate |
| **AniLumi** | High: anime schedules and watch orders are searched constantly | Combining two data sources into one-page answers | Days | Affiliates, ads |
| **Kundli Jyoti** | High: kundli, matching and rashifal searches in India | Computed charts plus honest positioning in a fear-driven category | About a week | ₹199 report, built but switched off |
| **RankFlowHQ Free Tools** | Medium: steady "free SEO tool" searches | Feeds RankFlowHQ, my paid product | Days | Indirect (Pro sign-ups) |
| **HarshOnRoads** | Medium: weekend-trip searches from Delhi NCR | My own 264+ travel Shorts as distribution | Ongoing | Travel affiliates |

## Sequencing: cheap signals first

I ranked launches by **time to signal**, not by how big the idea was:

1. **Quick launches first** (static or near-static sites): PayHours, then AniLumi, then Kundli Jyoti. Each went live within days on its own domain, with Search Console from day one.
2. **Turn on measurement before marketing:** search consoles and bot-free analytics, so the first decisions were based on real visitors.
3. **Only then go deeper:** more pages, features or payments, in whichever product earned it.

## What I decided not to build

Saying no was the most important part of the plan:

- **No payments before traction.** Kundli Jyoti's ₹199 report and Razorpay checkout are built but switched off. Charging before people return would hide whether they value it.
- **No accounts or databases unless needed.** Watchlists and family profiles live in the browser. That removes sign-up friction and server cost.
- **No ads on free hosting tiers.** Some free hosting plans don't allow commercial use, so ads and affiliates wait until a product has traffic and is on a plan that allows it.
- **No new idea that steals focus from one that's working.** New ideas had to beat an existing bet's next step, not just look exciting.

## What the data changed

Two products changed direction within days of launch because the data disagreed with my plan:

- **AniLumi:** I built for a global anime fan. Analytics and Bing searches showed a mobile audience in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh looking for **Hindi dubs**. The roadmap now starts with Hindi pages. → [AniLumi case study](/work/anilumi)
- **Kundli Jyoti:** the top searches were all for **Bhai Dooj** dates, and the festival page became the most visited page. Festival pages now go up weeks before each festival. → [Kundli Jyoti case study](/work/kundlijyoti)

<Callout type="tradeoff">
Spreading effort across several bets means no single product gets polished fast. I accepted that during the discovery phase. The next phase is to concentrate on the bets the data backs and stop investing in the rest.
</Callout>

## Mistakes in the strategy

- **I moved one product's hosting three times in ten days.** AniLumi's Google traffic dropped sharply on the day of the last move. Lesson: infrastructure choices are strategy too; pick the host before launch.
- **I treated Google as the only search engine at first.** Bing turned out to send real visitors. Every product now goes to both on day one.
- **I set up analytics late on some products.** PayHours still lacks bot-free visitor numbers, so I can't compare it fairly with the others yet.

## What's next

- Run each bet against a clear **keep / grow / stop** rule after 60 days of data: weekly visitors, return rate and earliest revenue signal.
- Double down on the strongest one or two, and stop adding features to the rest.
