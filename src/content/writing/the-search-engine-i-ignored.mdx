When I launched my first few side products, I did what most people do. I set up Google Search Console, submitted the sitemap and refreshed it every morning.

I didn't think much about Bing. That turned out to be a mistake, and also the most useful thing I learned this month.

## Week one: the Google high

[AniLumi](/work/anilumi) went from zero to about **90 clicks and 17,900 impressions a day** on Google in its first week. [PayHours](/work/payhourscalc), my US payroll calculator, went from zero to over 1,000 impressions a day.

I thought I had found the trick.

Then, within a few days of each other, both sites lost most of their Google impressions. AniLumi was also in the middle of moving hosts, and Google hit 316 pages with server errors. PayHours had no deploy at all and still dropped.

My honest reading now: **Google often gives a brand-new site a short burst of visibility to test it.** First-week numbers are a test, not a baseline. I read them as a trend, and that was wrong.

## Meanwhile, on Bing

While Google traffic was down, Bing started sending visitors. For AniLumi it went from almost nothing to **40–46 clicks a day** from early November:

| | Google (28 days) | Bing (3 months) |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks | 356 | 260 |
| Impressions | 71,900 | 6,900 |
| CTR | **0.5%** | **3.8%** |

Google showed AniLumi far more often, but almost nobody clicked. Bing showed it less, and people clicked about seven times as often.

And Bing's search terms told me who my users were. Almost every top query was about Hindi dubs: "anime dub hindi", "anime hindi dubbed", "anime in hindi". "Hindi dubbed anime" had a 26% click-through rate.

I had built AniLumi for a global anime fan. Bing told me my real user was a phone user in India looking for anime in Hindi. Analytics confirmed it a few days later. The roadmap now starts with Hindi.

<Callout type="insight">
Bing's audience is smaller, but in India a lot of it comes from Edge and default Windows search, and those users often click. Ignoring it means giving up easy, high-intent traffic.
</Callout>

## The same mistake twice

I'm a bit embarrassed by this one. Even after AniLumi taught me about Bing, I launched [Kundli Jyoti](/work/kundlijyoti) with only Google set up. Bing showed zero impressions for weeks. That's free traffic I left on the table because of habit.

Now it's on my launch checklist, and every one of my sites is verified on both.

## What I do now, on day one

- **Submit to Google and Bing on launch day.** Same sitemap, five extra minutes.
- **Don't judge a site on its first two weeks.** I wait for 60 days of data before deciding to keep, grow or stop.
- **Keep hosting stable while Google is judging you.** Moving hosts three times in ten days was my single worst decision this month.
- **Read the queries, not just the totals.** Search terms are the cheapest user research there is. People tell you exactly what they want.
- **Find pages that get seen but not clicked.** AniLumi's Eren Yeager page had 1,931 Google impressions and a 0.2% CTR. Fixing a title is much cheaper than writing a new page.
